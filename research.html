Working Papers

Partial Universalisation in Threshold Public Goods: Actions to Inclinations, 2026

This paper studies partial Kantian reasoning in finite threshold public-good environments. Under a probabilistic linkage between an individual's contemplated strategy and the behaviour of like-minded agents, the pure-contribution premium admits a collective-pivotality representation: it is the probability that the contribution-oriented counterfactual reaches the provision threshold while the abstention-oriented counterfactual does not.

I show that collective pivotality is strictly single-peaked in background cooperation for every admissible finite threshold, so that under intermediate contribution costs pure contribution is preferred only over a bounded range of support. Extending the model from deterministic commitments to endogenous contribution inclinations, I identify robust environments with a unique strict interior optimum, including cases in which pure abstention is preferred to pure contribution despite a positive optimal contribution inclination.

A Structural Theory of the Willingness-to-Pay Gap: Valuation and Behaviour in Climate Mitigation, 2026

UCL Journal of Economics

This paper distinguishes stated willingness to pay as a welfare-based valuation object from actual contribution as an optimisation-based behavioural choice. It shows that a value-action wedge can arise under stable preferences and rational choice because welfare valuation and contribution behaviour are governed by different economic conditions.

The model studies how altruism, warm-glow motives, social image, and observability affect the size of the wedge, and develops a limited-attention interpretation under which prompts emphasising budgets and opportunity costs can narrow the observed gap without changing preferences.

Other Writing

Towards a Theory of Explanatory Closure: A Map of the Boundary, 2026

This essay examines the limits and closure conditions of rational explanation from a metaphysical perspective, asking what kinds of explanatory stopping points are compatible with the possibility of rational inquiry.

Absurdity as Premise: On the Conditional Limits of Camus's Critique of "Philosophical Suicide", 2026

This essay argues that Camus's critique of "philosophical suicide" is conditional on accepting the absurd as the privileged starting point, and is therefore best understood as an internal critique rather than an external metacritique.

Who Speaks for the Philosopher King, 2026

This essay interprets medieval Christianity as a problem of mediated philosopher-kingship. It argues that the central difficulty lies in the reliability, corrigibility, and coercive power of those who claim to speak on its behalf.
